iOS 18+: `BleManager.ios.showAccessoryPicker(items, { timeoutMs })` shows AccessorySetupKit's picker with the nearby accessories that match `items` (an advertised service or company id, optionally a name substring, masked manufacturer or service data, and immediate range). The person's pick authorizes that accessory for the app without the Bluetooth permission prompt; the call resolves its `Peripheral`, or null when nothing was picked. The config plugin's new `ios.accessorySetupKit` option writes the Info.plist declarations (never an empty list: iOS stops the app on one), and the call rejects with `MISCONFIGURED` when an item uses an identifier they don't list or a list is malformed (iOS would stop the app). Declaring AccessorySetupKit changes how Bluetooth works for the whole app, measured on iOS 27.0.1: no prompt, scans see only picked accessories, and the adapter reads `poweredOff` until the first pick. In such an app the library uses the central manager without the prompt, and `getPermissionsAsync()` reports scanning and connecting as granted. See the new guide, `docs/guides/accessory-setup.md`. The testing fake picks the first advertising virtual peripheral that matches (`injectError` op `showAccessoryPicker`).
